Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic

Discover Sri Dalada Maligawa, one of Sri Lanka's most important Buddhist pilgrimage sites and a historic landmark at the heart of the Sacred City of Kandy.

About the Temple

The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic, commonly known as Sri Dalada Maligawa, stands within the historic royal palace complex of Kandy. It is renowned as the shrine associated with the Sacred Tooth Relic of the Buddha and remains an important Buddhist pilgrimage destination.

The temple is not simply a religious building. Its setting, architecture, rituals and connection with the former Kandyan kingdom make it one of the most significant cultural landmarks in Sri Lanka.

A Detailed History

The story of the Sacred Tooth Relic is closely connected with the history of Sri Lankan kingship, Buddhism and the changing capitals of the island.

The Sacred Tooth Relic and its Journey

According to the traditional account recorded by Sri Lankan Buddhist sources, the Sacred Tooth Relic was brought to Sri Lanka during the 4th century CE. Prince Dantha and Princess Hemamala are traditionally associated with bringing the relic to the island during the reign of King Keerthi Sri Meghavarna.

Over the centuries, the relic became closely associated with the authority of Sri Lankan rulers. Wherever the royal capital moved, the protection and veneration of the relic remained an important responsibility of the ruling kingdom.

The Tooth Relic and Sri Lankan Kingship

The Sacred Tooth Relic came to be regarded as a symbol of sovereignty. This explains why shrines associated with the relic were traditionally established close to royal residences. UNESCO's documentation identifies the long-standing relationship between the royal palace and the Temple of the Tooth as an important feature of Kandy's cultural heritage.

Kandy – The Last Royal Capital

Kandy became the last capital of the Sinhala kings. The city, historically known as Senkadagalapura, developed as a major political and religious centre surrounded by the mountains of Sri Lanka's central highlands.

UNESCO describes Kandy as the last capital of the Sinhala kings, whose patronage helped preserve and develop the island's traditional culture until the British occupation in 1815.

The Temple in the Kandyan Kingdom

Sri Lanka Tourism records that King Wimaladharmasuriya I built a temple for the Sacred Tooth Relic in Kandy in 1592. The building history of the temple continued under later Kandyan rulers, who expanded and rebuilt parts of the complex.

The Royal Palace Complex

The temple formed part of a larger royal complex rather than standing as an isolated shrine. The relationship between the palace and the temple reflects an important tradition in which the Sacred Tooth Relic was closely connected with royal power and religious legitimacy.

Paththirippuwa – The Octagon

One of the most recognisable structures associated with the complex is the Paththirippuwa, or Octagon. Sri Lanka Tourism attributes its construction to King Sri Wickrama Rajasinghe. The structure originally formed part of the royal palace complex and later became part of the temple.

Kandyan Architecture

The temple complex contains architectural features associated with the Kandyan tradition. The entrance area includes the Mahawahalkada and a traditionally carved Sandakadapahana, while the surrounding structures contain decorative details reflecting the artistic traditions of the Kandyan period.

The Sacred Chamber

The Sacred Tooth Relic is kept within a highly protected inner chamber. Sri Lanka Tourism describes the relic as being enclosed within a series of protective caskets decorated with precious materials.

Daily Religious Rituals

Daily rituals form an important part of the life of the temple. These ceremonies, traditionally accompanied by drumming and other forms of religious observance, continue the long-standing tradition of honouring the Sacred Tooth Relic.

The Esala Perahera

Kandy is also internationally known for the Esala Perahera, the great annual procession associated with the Sacred Tooth Relic. The procession brings together traditional dancers, drummers, ceremonial performers and richly decorated elephants, creating one of Sri Lanka's most recognisable cultural events.

The Colonial Period

Kandy remained the last independent royal capital until the British occupation in 1815. Even after the end of the Kandyan monarchy, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic retained its importance as a centre of Buddhist devotion and Sri Lankan cultural identity.

The Temple in the Modern Era

Today, Sri Dalada Maligawa continues to function as an active place of worship and pilgrimage. It is also one of the most important cultural attractions in Kandy, visited by pilgrims and travellers from Sri Lanka and around the world.

UNESCO World Heritage Significance

The Sacred City of Kandy was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1988. UNESCO recognises the city's historic royal ensemble, its Buddhist significance and its connection with the Temple of the Tooth as important elements of its outstanding cultural value.

Visitor Information

👕

Dress Respectfully

As this is a sacred Buddhist site, visitors should wear respectful clothing. Avoid clothing that is unsuitable for a religious place.

👣

Remove Shoes

Visitors should follow the temple's rules regarding footwear when entering designated sacred areas.

🙏

Respect Worshippers

Keep noise low and respect people who are praying, making offerings or taking part in religious ceremonies.

Best Time to Visit

The best time depends on what you want to experience. Visitors interested in the temple's daily religious atmosphere may prefer a time when ceremonies are taking place. Those looking for a quieter sightseeing experience should consider visiting outside the busiest periods.

During the annual Kandy Esala Perahera period, the city becomes especially busy and the atmosphere is very different from an ordinary day.
